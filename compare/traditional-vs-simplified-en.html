Traditional vs Simplified Chinese: What's the Difference?

Beginner台湾華語Read in another language: 日本語 · 한국어

Traditional and simplified are two written forms of the same Chinese language — they're not different languages or dialects. Taiwan, Hong Kong, and Macau use traditional characters; mainland China and Singapore use simplified. Here's what actually differs, and which one you should learn.

Why Taiwan Uses Traditional Characters

Traditional Chinese characters are the form that developed continuously from earlier Chinese script, largely unchanged in structure for the last several centuries. When the government in mainland China simplified thousands of characters starting in the 1950s to boost literacy, Taiwan did not adopt the changes, so it kept using traditional forms in schools, media, and government documents.

How the Two Systems Differ

Simplification mostly reduced stroke counts and merged some characters that used to be written differently. The core vocabulary and grammar of Mandarin didn't change — a traditional-reading learner and a simplified-reading learner are reading the same language, just different character shapes. Some characters are identical in both systems (人, 大, 中), while others look quite different (見/见, 東/东, 國/国).

Which One Should You Learn

If your goal is Taiwan — travel, media, family, or work — learn traditional characters, since that's what you'll see everywhere in daily life there. If you already know simplified, the good news is that traditional characters are largely learnable through recognition rather than starting over, since the underlying language is identical.
